How it works

  1. Tell us what's coming up. A procedure, a trip, or getting ready for winter.
  2. Choose what helps. Free services and simple steps first. You say yes to each one.
  3. Print your fridge card. One page with who to call and what's planned.

Our goal

Help people who live alone stay independent, with a simple plan made before anything happens.

Our mission

Free local services and simple steps come first. Friends come in only if you choose. No pressure, nothing to buy, and nothing is ever sent for you.

Why we built this

Many people who live alone have a quiet worry: what happens if I need a hand? A ride home after a procedure, someone to feed the cat, a way for help to get in. These are small things, but they are hard to plan for alone.

We built Just in Case to make that planning calm and simple. It leans on free local services first, asks before every step, and never pressures anyone. You stay in charge of your own plan.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. Just in Case is free to use. Some services it suggests have a cost. Free ones always come first.

Who can see my information?

Only you, unless you choose to share. What you type is sent to an AI service so it can write replies. Nothing is kept after your visit unless you tap "Save my progress" (saved only on your device) or "Share with family" (your helper list only, with first names, deleted after 30 days). We never sell data.

I'm not good with technology. Can I still use it?

Yes. You can type, or tap Speak and talk. You can make the text bigger and have answers read aloud. Nothing happens until you say yes. A friend or family member can sit with you, too.

What if I don't have anyone to ask?

That's okay. Your plan can use services and simple steps only. Boston's Age Strong Commission (Boston's Council on Aging) and Mass 211 can help you find more.

Is this an emergency service?

No. In an emergency, always call 911. Just in Case is a planning tool. It does not watch over you, and it cannot call anyone.

Just in Case will never ask for money, bank details, or your Social Security number.

            Going away? Hold your mail

            The Post Office keeps your mail safe for 3 to 30 days. It's free.

            1. Sign in to USPS.com, or make a free account.
            2. Confirm who you are. If USPS mails you a code, it takes 3 to 5 days, so start a week or more early.

            What Just in Case is doing

              Before we share your list

              Just in Case does not save anything unless you say yes. To share, we need to save your helper list.

              • In an emergency, always call 911. Updates on this list, like who signed up, are not urgent.
              • What we save: only your helper list (tasks, dates, times, and names). Not your chat, card, contacts, or medications.
              • Who can see it: anyone you give the link to.
              • How long: it is deleted after 30 days, or right away when you stop sharing.
              • What helpers can do: add their own name to an open task, or remove it. The list shows first names only.
