About Features Make my plan

Make a small backup plan, and a card for your fridge, in case you need a hand while living alone.

Our goal

Help people who live alone stay independent, with a simple plan made before anything happens.

Our mission

Free local services and simple steps come first. Friends come in only if you choose. No pressure, nothing to buy, and nothing is ever sent for you.

How it works

  1. Tell us what's coming up. A procedure, a trip, or getting ready for winter.
  2. Choose what helps. Free services and simple steps first. You say yes to each one.
  3. Print your fridge card. One page with who to call and what's planned.

What Just in Case can do

  • Plain-language chat

    Tell it what's coming up, in your own words.

  • Speak or type

    Tap Speak to talk instead of typing. You check the words before you send.

  • Read aloud

    Any answer can be read to you, in the voice you pick.

  • Free services first

    Boston services, free ones first, each with the month it was checked.

  • You approve every step

    Nothing goes into your plan until you say yes.

  • Fridge card

    One page with 911 at the top, and only the details you choose.

  • Print my plan

    Your whole plan on one clean page, to keep or hand to someone.

  • Recaps as you go

    A short "So far, we've planned" after each part, so nothing gets lost.

  • Save and come back

    Only if you choose. Saved in this browser on this device, and you can delete it any time.

  • Planning for someone else

    For a parent, relative, or friend, once they agree. They decide what goes in it.

  • No one to ask? That's okay.

    A plan with services only, plus Boston's Council on Aging and Mass 211.

  • Helper table

    List things you'd like help with. Family can sign up for open tasks.

  • Share with family

    Only if you agree. First names only, and you can stop sharing any time.

  • Helper updates

    In an emergency, call 911 first. These are calm, non-urgent updates, like when a helper signs up or steps back.

  • Hold your mail

    Step-by-step help with free USPS Hold Mail.

  • People you can talk to

    988 and other free support lines, any time of day.

  • Easy to see

    Bigger text, and light or dark mode.

  • Never asks for money

    No money, bank details, or Social Security number. Ever.

          Going away? Hold your mail

          The Post Office keeps your mail safe for 3 to 30 days. It's free.

          1. Sign in to USPS.com, or make a free account.
          2. Confirm who you are. If USPS mails you a code, it takes 3 to 5 days, so start a week or more early.
          3. Pick the days you'll be away.
          Go to USPS Hold Mail

          This opens the official USPS website in a new tab. You sign in there. Just in Case never sees your password.

          What Just in Case is doing

            Before we share your list

            Just in Case does not save anything unless you say yes. To share, we need to save your helper list.

            • In an emergency, always call 911. Updates on this list, like who signed up, are not urgent.
            • What we save: only your helper list (tasks, dates, times, and names). Not your chat, card, contacts, or medications.
            • Who can see it: anyone you give the link to.
            • How long: it is deleted after 30 days, or right away when you stop sharing.
            • What helpers can do: add their own name to an open task, or remove it. The list shows first names only.
